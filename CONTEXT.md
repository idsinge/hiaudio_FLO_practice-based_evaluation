## What This Repository Is

This repository publishes findings and methodology from a practice-based evaluation of the Hi-Audio web audio platform (hiaudio.fr), conducted by the Female Laptop Orchestra (FLO) between February and March 2026. The evaluation was carried out as part of the Hi-AUDiO research project, funded by the European Research Council (ERC) under Horizon Europe (grant No. 101052978).

The repository is public and hosted on GitHub Pages. It is intended to support open dissemination and reproducibility for other researchers.

**Repo:** https://github.com/idsinge/hiaudio_FLO_practice-based_evaluation
**GitHub Pages:** https://idsinge.github.io/hiaudio_FLO_practice-based_evaluation/

## Key Design Decisions

### Item attribution and validity language

**Corrected 2026-08-20.** This section previously said the questionnaire only "adapts constructs" and that named instruments including TAM must never be cited. Item-level comparison against the primary sources showed that premise was factually wrong, and it had already produced an under-attribution error in README.md. Two things were being conflated — *where the items came from* and *whether the assembled instrument is validated* — and they must be stated separately.

**Provenance — say it plainly.** The items *are* largely drawn from published technology-acceptance scales:

- **Q18** = the four-item **Perceived Ease of Use** scale as worded in Venkatesh (2000, Appendix 1), verbatim except the tense is shifted present→past and "the system" becomes "the Hi-Audio platform"; Q18_2 also drops "my" ("a lot of my mental effort" → "a lot of mental effort") (found by an external review, 2026-11-06).
- **Q19** = the three-item **Perceived Enjoyment** scale from the same appendix, same treatment.
- **Q20_2** = Davis (1989) perceived-usefulness item, domain changed from "in my job" to "for recording music".
- **Q20_1 / Q20_3** = attitude and behavioural-intention items in the TAM tradition whose **direct wording source has not been established** — do not attribute them to Davis (1989), which developed only the usefulness and ease-of-use scales. **Q20_4** (recommendation), **Q16** and **Q17** were written for this study.

Cite Venkatesh (2000) for the item wording, Davis (1989) for the constructs, and Davis, Bagozzi & Warshaw (1992) as the upstream origin of the enjoyment items. Never write "no items are reproduced" — a false attribution claim is a worse error than overclaiming.

**Validity — the original caution stands.** The instrument *as assembled* is not a standardised questionnaire and was never re-validated: items were recombined across sources and n = 9 cannot establish reliability. Keep two things separate — the **verb tense** was changed present→past, and the **framing** moved from a system in current use to one recalled months later. No comparison against published norms, no treating block means as validated subscale scores, results descriptive only.

**Unaffected and still true:** none of SUS, NASA-TLX, the USE Questionnaire, AttrakDiff or UEQ was administered in this study, and none was used as a source. Do not name any of them as instruments used here. Be precise about how far the claim goes. For **SUS and NASA-TLX** the stronger version also holds — none of their items appear, their wording being distinctive, and both belong to the companion 22-participant study. For **USE** it does not: Lund's questionnaire contains "It is easy to use", "I would recommend it to a friend", "It is fun to use" and "It is pleasant to use", close enough to Q18_3, Q20_4, Q19_3 and Q19_2 that a categorical no-overlap claim cannot be defended. That resemblance comes from a shared construct vocabulary, not from USE.

This applies to all public-facing documents. The full annotated attribution table is in `docs/REFERENCES.md`; §1 of the analysis carries the reader-facing version.

## Analysis Approach

The analysis is exploratory and descriptive. Inferential statistics are not planned given the expected small sample size.

- **Q1–Q15:** frequency counts, percentages, narrative sample description
- **Q16–Q20:** means, medians, range
- **Q21–Q23:** thematic coding — hybrid deductive/inductive approach
  - Deductive frame: §4 (Platform Strengths) and §5 (Workflow Challenges) of the memo (`docs/output.md`)
  - Inductive: open to new themes not in the existing framework
- **Triangulation:** survey findings compared against the practice-based memo to identify confirmation, nuance, contradiction, and new issues

Q17 sits at the boundary — report its mean alongside Q16, Q18–Q20, but also read it qualitatively alongside Q21 and Q22 when interpreting artistic fit.
